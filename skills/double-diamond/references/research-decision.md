# Research and decision tasks

Load this for "should we", "which approach", build-versus-buy, strategy, trade-off analysis, and open-ended research questions.

## Discover before asking

Gather what already exists: internal documents, prior decisions, numbers the user has supplied, and current external facts via search. Establish the decision's real constraints (budget, deadline, who decides, what is already committed) from sources before asking.

## Decisions that usually matter

- **The decision criteria and their weights.** This is often the genuinely open question, and it is a values call only the user can make. Ask it when the top options differ mainly on a trade-off the user has not settled.
- **Time horizon and reversibility.** A reversible decision deserves less analysis than a one-way door.
- **Who is affected and who decides.**
- **What evidence would change the answer.**

## Baseline additions for research

- **Source quality and recency.** Prefer primary sources and current data; say how old a figure is.
- **Counter-evidence.** Actively look for the strongest case against the leading option and report it.
- **Calibrated uncertainty.** Separate what is established, what is likely, and what is a guess. Give a confidence level.
- **Base rates.** Compare to how such decisions usually turn out before adjusting for specifics.
- Do not present a single study, vendor claim, or anecdote as settled.

## Lenses for Phase 2 (examples; derive real ones from the forks)

Optimize for speed to learning; for lowest downside; for lowest long-run cost; for keeping options open. Strong candidates steelman their option. The judge asks what evidence would flip the ranking, and the cheapest test of it becomes part of the plan.

## Pre-mortem prompts

If this decision turns out badly in a year, why? What was the assumption we never tested? What did we weight too lightly? What is the cheapest experiment that would have revealed it?

## Plan shape

Recommendation first; then confidence and the one or two things that would change it; then the cheapest next test; then the alternatives rejected, one line each. Keep the analysis behind the recommendation unless asked.
